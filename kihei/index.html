Wastewater

The cesspool clock

Hawaiʻi has ~83,000 cesspools (~14,000 on Kauaʻi) and passed Act 125 in 2017 requiring every one to be converted, upgraded, or sewer-connected by 2050. There is no automatic upgrade-on-sale trigger statewide today, but Priority-1 cesspools (near water) can be ordered converted sooner — and the east side of Kauaʻi is water-adjacent.

  • Cost: ~$20k–$40k for a conventional septic; ~$20k–$50k for an ATU. A steep terraced lot with tight access commonly forces the pricier ATU and pushes to the top of the range.
  • Tax credit: the old up-to-$10,000 state credit sunset in 2020 and is not available in 2026. Reinstatement bills (e.g., HB2079) are pending but not law — don't budget for it.
  • Diligence: confirm the cesspool's location/condition and whether the parcel is in a Priority-1 zone during inspection; that determines whether this is a 2040s problem or a sooner one.

Treat this as a known future capital cost of ~$30–50k. It's a legitimate line in your offer math, and a fair thing to ask the seller to credit.

Non-standard terms & drafting defects, ranked

C-1 · No financing contingency on a financed purchase

Fix before signing

The form requires electing H-1 (no cash contingency) or H-2, and separately offers H-3, the Financing Contingency. This draft checks H-1 and leaves H-3 unchecked — on RR201, an unchecked paragraph is not part of the contract — even though C-2 says $798,400 comes "by way of 30 year mortgage." If the loan dies, you don't get to exit under O-2; you're in default under O-1, where the seller may keep the deposits or elect to sue for damages (deposit retention is an election in this form, not a cap). Standard practice on every financed Hawaiʻi deal is H-3 checked. Zack's own email said "we are contingent on financing" — the draft doesn't deliver that.

Exposure: deposits + open-ended damages claim if the loan fails — including failing because the appraiser flags the unpermitted bathroom.

C-2 · No appraisal protection either — H-4's deadlines are blank

Fix before signing

The RR201 has no standalone appraisal contingency — the only appraisal protection in the whole form lives in H-4(b)(ii) ("evidence that an appraisal… satisfactory to Lender has been received"), which only operates with H-3. Here H-4 is checked but its three deadline blanks (prequal due date, conditional-approval date, conditions-satisfied date) are all empty, making it unenforceable scaffolding. On a house at $605/sqft with 115 DOM, walking in with zero appraisal protection is the single riskiest election in this draft.

Fix: check H-3 + fill H-4's three deadlines (e.g., prequal attached; conditional approval ≥ 5 days before closing).

C-3 · Sellers on the contract ≠ the name on every operating document

Verify

The contract sellers are Lori Orr & Richard Orr. But the Sunrun lease (Nov 2023), every KIUC bill through July 2026, and the permit-history email are all Andrew M. Locke. Someone's relationship to this property is unexplained — and it matters concretely: the Sunrun Service Transfer Agreement must be initiated by the actual lessee (Sunrun wants ~30 days' notice before closing), and the sellers' 508D disclosure duty is limited to what they actually know — non-occupant sellers know less. The seller-residency boxes (Foreign / Non-resident / Owner-occupant) on page 14 are also unchecked, so HARPTA status is unknown.

Ask: who holds title, who is the Sunrun lessee, and who occupied the home? The preliminary title report will answer #1 — don't wait for it to ask.

C-4 · Section Q is empty — every negotiation lever left unused

Deliberate?

Full asking price, zero seller concessions, and no special terms at all: no requirement that the seller produce the permit certificate/stamped plans for 05-1859, no Sunrun buyout or assumption-approval condition, no cesspool credit — against a listing with 115 DOM and a prior $102k cut. If offering full price clean is a deliberate strategy to win the deal, fine — but it should be a decision, not a default. At minimum, the permit-paperwork condition costs nothing and protects the loan.

This dossier's plan: ~$930k open · seller solar buyout · cesspool credit — the draft does none of the three.

C-5 · Termite contingency is half-drafted

Drafting defect

L-2's blanks are filled (3 days, buyer selects operator, report 15 days before closing, $1,000 cap) but the L-2 paragraph checkbox itself is unchecked — arguably not incorporated at all — and the "who pays" boxes (Buyer/Seller) are both unchecked. Hawaiʻi custom on resales: the seller pays for the termite inspection report (and the form already makes the seller pay for treatment if infestation is found). A 1956 wood home is exactly where you want this airtight.

Fix: check the L-2 box, mark Seller as payer — that's the customary allocation, not a concession.

C-6 · Sunrun assumption isn't a closing condition

Tighten

E-5 is properly used (lease identified, docs in 3 days, 15-day rescission after receipt) — that's currently your only solar protection, and it expires mid-escrow. Standard practice for leased-solar deals is to also make the buyer's obligation contingent on Sunrun approving the Service Transfer before closing. Without it, you could reach Oct 19 with the transfer unapproved and no exit. (Sunrun's process: seller initiates, buyer passes a soft credit check, ~30 days' notice.)

Fix: Q-term making closing contingent on approved Sunrun transfer — or on the seller's lease payoff, per the dossier's recommendation.

C-7 · Housekeeping items

Minor

(a) The Lead-Based Paint Disclosure addendum is checked in D-1 but isn't in the packet — it's federally required for a 1956 home; confirm it's attached and signed before or with the contract. (b) G-2(c)'s title-defect cure period is blank. (c) The M-1 document windows are "0" days — moot with no HOA, but sloppy. (d) Buyer cash-evidence (H-1b) due in 2 days via lender verification — tight but fine.

All fixable in one markup pass with Zack before signing.

What's standard and correctly drafted

Matches Hawaiʻi custom
  • Closing-cost splits per the printed F-6 chart: title 60/40 seller/buyer, escrow 50/50, seller pays conveyance tax, staking, FIRPTA/HARPTA mechanics.
  • Seller-paid staking (K-1, 21 days) + encroachment termination right (K-3).
  • 508D disclosures at full statutory strength: 7-day delivery (faster than the 10-day default), 15-day rescission intact.
  • Mediation-first dispute path, Hawaiʻi law, Kauaʻi forum — no arbitration substitution.
Buyer-favorable elections
  • 21-day inspection window (custom is 10–14) — enough for structural, cesspool, and permit review.
  • No "As Is" addendum — repair/maintenance obligations (J-3/J-4, 150% holdback) fully intact.
  • $1,000 cleaning credit at closing (J-9b); trash-out 3 days before closing (J-8).
  • 1% earnest money, no additional deposit scheduled — light exposure (a counter may push this up).
Bottom line on the draft The cost splits and boilerplate are clean, standard Hawaiʻi. The problem is concentrated in one place: the financing architecture (H-3/H-4) is switched off on a mortgage-financed purchase of a property with a known appraisal risk (the unpermitted bathroom), and the deal-specific protections this dossier recommended (permit paperwork, Sunrun transfer/buyout, cesspool) are absent from Section Q. One markup pass fixes all of it: check H-3, fill H-4's dates, check & assign L-2 to seller, add Q-1 (permit docs), Q-2 (Sunrun transfer approval or payoff), attach the lead-paint addendum, and get the Orr-vs-Locke question answered in writing — then sign.
