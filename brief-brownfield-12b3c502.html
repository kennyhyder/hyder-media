The company

What brownfield.ai actually is — and where the datacenter story is real vs. veneer.

brownfield.ai — environmental due-diligence SaaS ~6 months old

An address-lookup tool that scans public + private environmental records and returns a property risk report — "weeks of manual research into minutes." Native market is brownfield real estate: brokers, developers, lenders, environmental consultants, and economic-development orgs.

What it does

  • Deep Search — address → environmental risk report from EPA/ECHO, CERCLIS/Superfund, LUST/UST tanks, PFAS indicators, FEMA flood zones, state cleanup records, zoning/permits. Text/report-based, not map-based.
  • Data Rooms — secure document sharing (Phase I/II ESAs, remediation records).
  • Listings — controlled brownfield property listings. Knowledge Base — per-property dashboard.

The datacenter angle veneer, not substance

They run dedicated "Data Center" and "Energy & Infrastructure" landing pages with the right buzzwords ("power, fiber, redundancy, speed to execution"). But the product explicitly disclaims utility/power studies and delivers no transmission, interconnection-queue, or speed-to-power data. It surfaces environmental red flags; it does not answer "is this site good for a datacenter, and how good, ranked." This is your single strongest wedge.

Where they're genuinely strong

  • Real environmental-risk depth (EPA/CERCLIS/LUST/PFAS/flood) — a niche you're thinner on.
  • A deal-room / listings / collaboration workflow you don't have.
  • Clean SMB pricing + self-serve onboarding.
  • Disciplined SEO content engine (~20 posts in 6 months) — they get your playbook.

Where they're thin / exposed

  • No power / grid / interconnection data — the decisive DC layer.
  • No disclosed scale (vs. your 147k scored sites); on-demand lookups, not a pre-scored national universe.
  • Gated & address-based — you can't discover sites, only look up ones you have.
  • Early & unproven: no funding/press, no named customers, no team/About page (404).
  • $150–$500/mo pricing signals SMB brokers, not hyperscaler/enterprise budgets.

Meeting playbook

Posture, questions, and what to guard.

Posture

  • Warm, peer-to-peer, curious. You've dodged his last few asks, so he's clearly keen — you hold the scarcity. Don't oversell; let him articulate what he wants first.
  • Lead with complementarity, not competition. "Your environmental depth + my power/scored-site breadth is the complete screen" is the frame that opens every good option.
  • Anchor your value to the category, quietly. Mention the LandGate / datacenterHawk consolidation as context, not as a demand.
  • Guard the method. Talk vision and outcomes; be vague on how your pipeline, scoring, and SEO engine actually work. He has every incentive to learn it.

Questions to ask him (public info is thin — make him fill the gaps)

  1. Is brownfield.ai funded, or bootstrapped inside Percent Invisible? Any raise planned?
  2. Who's actually using the datacenter product today — any named DC/energy customers or pilots?
  3. How are you sourcing power/grid data, if at all — or is that the piece you're looking to solve?
  4. What's the vision — environmental DD tool, or a full site-selection platform? Where does it stop?
  5. Any acquirer or channel relationships already in motion (Enverus, S&P, Wood Mac, brokers)?

Options to have ready to float (in escalating order)

  • Data swap pilot — trade a slice of each other's data on a shared set of sites; see if the combined screen is compelling. Cheap, fast, reveals fit.
  • License the GridCensus power/scored-site feed into his reports (annual, with refresh + support).
  • Referral loop — your public funnel → his paid workflow; his brownfield leads → your DC scoring.
  • Bigger only if he's funded — dataset acquisition or merge, on category-anchored terms with cash weighting, not paper.
