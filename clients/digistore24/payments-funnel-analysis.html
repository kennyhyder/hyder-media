Payments Funnel — Competitive Reality Check

DS24 leadership asked what Whop, CheckoutChamp, and Shop are doing on Google Ads to win businesses searching for payment processing. This analyzes their complete paid-search footprints (SimilarWeb, trailing 12 months) plus the real Keyword Planner market for payment-processing terms — so the strategy conversation starts from what these brands actually do, not what they appear to do.

Sources: SimilarWeb paid keywords (worldwide, 2025-08 → 2026-07) · Google Keyword Planner (US) · compiled 2026-08-26

The Headline

None of the three reference brands wins payment-processing searches. Each runs a completely different playbook.

Whop (~$638K/yr est. paid): 88.3% of paid clicks are its own brand name — demand created on TikTok/YouTube/Discord, harvested on Google. Another 11.3% is bidding on its own sellers' product names (buyer capture for hosted communities). Genuine payment-processing terms: 1 keyword, 60 clicks — 0.006%.

Shop.app (~$5.2M/yr est. paid): it's Shopify's consumer shopping app — 59% consumer product queries ("purple jeans", "new balance 9060"), 38% navigational brand. Payment-processing terms: 0.004%. Not a B2B lead-gen play at all.

CheckoutChamp (~$39K/yr est. paid — tiny): the only genuinely B2B footprint. 57.5% competitor-conquest (shopify, clickfunnels, samcart), 16.5% checkout/CRO terms, and still only 1.3% payment-processing core.

Implication: the "Whop does this successfully" premise inverts — Whop's search program is proof that this audience is won off Google and defended on it. The searchable payment-processing market exists (below) but is priced for dedicated processors, not platforms.

Strategic read for the sales conversation

1. Head terms are a knife fight: "merchant services" / "credit card processing" run $22–104 top-of-page against Stripe, Square, and banks — and the searcher skews new sellers without volume, the opposite of the stated ICP. At DS24's ~$130 vendor-signup value, $25+ CPCs need conversion rates no cold funnel delivers.

2. The defensible slices are the DS24-shaped ones: high-risk/supplement processing (supplements are a DS24 specialty; "high risk merchant account" 1.9K/mo), digital-product-specific terms ("payment processor for digital products/courses/coaches"), "merchant of record", and "stripe alternatives" positioned for digital sellers. Small volumes, real fit.

3. The genuinely stealable play is Whop's, and it isn't payment terms: bidding on your own marketplace sellers' brand and product names to capture buyer demand. DS24 has thousands of vendors with searchable offers — that's GMV growth and a vendor-facing value prop ("we buy your brand traffic for you"), and nobody is contesting those terms.
