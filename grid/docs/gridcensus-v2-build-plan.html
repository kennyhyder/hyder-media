Bottom line

Jon handed you a product roadmap from the exact seat you're building for. Two modes matter to him: "I need a site" (discovery — what GridCensus does today) and "someone brought me a site, vet it" (new). Across both, his priorities rank: (1) speed-to-power reality incl. utility progress, (2) power bottlenecks / captive power, (3) size-aware regulatory support, (4) the DC pipeline + who the off-taker is. Almost everything he asked for is buildable on free public data — the hard part isn't access, it's calibration and honesty.

But first, fix the credibility bug. Both Jon and Kyle (brownfield.ai) independently sensed the scoring is off because Texas ranks low — and they're right. It's a concrete inversion in the code (below). Nothing else you build lands with a sophisticated user until Texas ranks where every analyst puts it: at the top. Fix that in Phase 0, then build.

The plan — phased

Phase 0 is the trust fix. Phase 1 is Jon's "buildable this week" list. Phase 2 is the differentiators. Phase 3 is moat.

0

Fix the scoring credibility bug ✓ DONE — shipped 2026-08-26

Shipped & verified

v5 scoring live: replaced the queue-depth penalty with an ISO-keyed speed-to-power base (ERCOT 92 … PJM 34), reweighted speed 15%→20%, rescored all 164k sites, and synced the on-site score breakdown + methodology + api-docs + home copy. Result (live in DB): Texas rank 36→12 of 47; top TX site 71.5 (speed 84); Georgia #2, Virginia 34, Ohio 20, California last — matches CBRE/JLL market reality. The methodology page now states plainly that Texas ranks at the top.

The exact bug

score-dc-sites.py → score_speed_to_power() computes depth_score = linear_score(queue_depth, 0, 30) — "30+ queued projects = 0." ERCOT has the deepest interconnection queue in the US, so Texas gets zeroed on 20% of its speed-to-power score. But ERCOT's deep queue is a demand signal, not a slowness signal: its connect-and-manage model energizes in ~1.5–3 yrs vs 5–7 in PJM, at ~$29/kW vs PJM's ~$240/kW, and it brought 14.2 GW online in 2021–22 vs PJM's 5.6. The score has the single most important siting factor inverted.

Rip out the queue-depth penalty → ISO-keyed speed-to-power
Replace the depth penalty with a market-level speed-to-power factor: ERCOT = fastest (top), MISO/SPP = mid, PJM = slowest, non-ISO case-by-case. Reinterpret queue depth as a positive liquidity signal or drop it — never zero out a fast market. Re-weight toward what CBRE/JLL/datacenterHawk actually price: speed-to-power ~35% · power price/availability ~25% · regulatory/siting ~20% · land ~15% · fiber/water ~5%. Then --rescore all 164k sites.
Sanity check: after rescoring, West Texas / Abilene must rank high (CBRE calls Abilene a tertiary market outpacing primaries). If a PJM N-Virginia parcel outranks an ERCOT parcel on speed-to-power, it's still miscalibrated.
Make the score defensible, not just correct

Kyle and Jon distrusted a black-box number. Every scored site should show its components ("speed-to-power 82: ERCOT connect-and-manage, load growth +6%, brownfield grid bonus") so a developer can agree or argue. Explainability is the credibility feature.

The differentiators (2–4 wks)

Captive-power / bottleneck screen medium the flagship
A per-site Captive Power flag: co-locate three free signals — (a) a large generator nearby (EIA-860, with interconnection kV), (b) low utilization (EIA-923 generation ÷ nameplate = capacity factor) or measured curtailment, (c) an export constraint present (ERCOT SCED binding constraints — you already ingest these; other ISOs via persistent nodal LMP spread). Surface components, not a fake number: "~100 MW likely captive: 350 MW plant, 61% CF, node $18/MWh below load hub 40% of hours, 138 kV tie." This is Jon's captive-power concept, made real.
Data (free): gridstatus OSS lib (nodal LMP + congestion, all 7 ISOs, no key), EIA-860/923 via PUDL (clean joins), ERCOT NP6-86-CD SCED constraints + curtailment (have), CAISO/SPP direct curtailment. Ship ERCOT + CAISO + SPP first (best data); extend to PJM/MISO/NYISO/ISO-NE; flag non-ISO Southeast/West as weak.
DC pipeline v2 — under-construction + queue + off-taker medium
Layer in under-construction/announced projects and datacenter interconnection queue. Key nuance: only ERCOT tags "data center" load (NPRR-1267 Large Load report, monthly — but aggregated to Load-Zone/TSP, not lat-lng; ~233 GW under study, >70% datacenters). LBNL "Queued Up" and gridstatus queues are generation-only — they do NOT cover load. So: site-score datacenter load inside ERCOT; everywhere else show only regional GW-in-pipeline with a source link. Attach off-taker/tenant where public.
Data: pipeline spine = Epoch AI Frontier DC Hub (CC-BY, direct CSV, coords + IT-power MW + phases, daily) + dcmap.us (API, 307 UC / 751 planned) + Cleanview (free tier). New-project firehose = Business Wire / DCD RSS + SEC EDGAR full-text (public devs incl. Hut 8). ERCOT queue = NPRR-1267 + ercotqueue.com (CC-BY, county-level, generation). Off-taker = Texas Comptroller DC Registry (names owner+occupant+operator, XLSX) + DataCentersExposed.com (free shell-LLC unmasking, 1,343 codenames) + DIY assessor→SoS→SEC Exhibit-21 chain. Skip Acres (enterprise, no free API).
Industrial-repurposing layer have data
Promote heavy-power legacy industrial sites (steel/aluminum/smelting) that already hold large interconnections/permits into a first-class filter — a hot conversion theme Jon raised. Validate completeness of your existing set against EIA retired-generator + EPA heavy-industry data and flag confidence.
Data (free, public-domain): DOE Sept-2024 coal-site study (145 coal sites; Table 6 = 27 retired ≥600 MW, reusable switchyards) + EIA-860M (monthly retirements) / EIA-860 (lat/lon) via PUDL + EPA GHGRP/FLIGHT (steel Subpart Q, aluminum Subpart F, lat/lon+NAICS) + USGS aluminum-smelter list. Join on EPA FRS Registry ID. Validate completeness by cross-checking 3 universes (EIA retirements, GHGRP heavy-industry, DOE list) vs trade-press. Proof case: Century Hawesville KY → TeraWulf → ~401 MW Anthropic lease.
Utility posture → into the score medium
Fold large-load tariff terms (threshold, take-or-pay %, collateral, curtailment/BYO-gen mandates) into the regulatory score per utility service territory — the closest public proxy for "has the utility gotten the joke / how hard will they make this."
Data (free): SEPA DELTa + EEI large-load list (monthly). Map utility → county by service territory.

Moat (ongoing)

Persistent-congestion nodal analysis (all 7 ISOs) · News→sentiment LLM pass · Off-taker unmasking
Deepen the captive-power signal into a trailing-12-mo persistent-congestion metric per node; run a weekly cheap-LLM pass over GDELT/local-gov feeds to produce a live per-county "latest hurdles" summary + sentiment; expand off-taker/entity resolution. These are the compounding-data advantages the gated incumbents have — built free and public.

What you can't fully deliver (say so in the UI)

  • Actual utility conversations (Jon's #1) are mostly non-public. Approximate with tariff posture + queue entries + news; never claim to know a private negotiation. Position sites as "worth a real interconnection study," not "shovel-ready."
  • Exact captive MW everywhere needs paid deliverability data (ERIS/NRIS limits). Report measured curtailment where it exists (CAISO/SPP/ERCOT); elsewhere show the pattern, flagged as qualitative.
  • Non-ISO Southeast & West (vertically integrated, no nodal LMP) → the bottleneck feature is materially weaker there. Flag the region, lean on WECC paths + OASIS ATC.
  • Off-taker is ~50/50 public (hyperscalers hide behind shell LLCs). Default to the hyperscaler-footprint fallback + Texas Comptroller registry + DataCentersExposed.
  • Datacenter load queue is ERCOT-only and aggregated to Load-Zone/TSP (no lat-lng); elsewhere show regional GW-in-pipeline, not per-site.
Licensing discipline (public tool)

Only CC-BY / ODbL / public-domain sources are safe to redistribute: Epoch AI, PNNL/OSM, Bommar moratorium data, ercotqueue, LBNL, and all EPA/EIA/USGS/SEC/Census/FCC/GDELT. Proprietary trackers (dcmap, Cleanview, Baxtel, datacenterHawk, ZoneWire) are leads only — verify against primary sources and store the source URL per row so every GridCensus record is independently attributable. Bake a browser-grade User-Agent + polite throttling for bot-blocked hosts (DCD, datacentermap 429).

Being explicit about these is the credibility move — it's what separates a screening tool a developer trusts from a black box he doesn't.

Where this leaves you

This roadmap turns GridCensus from a scored directory into a siting workbench that serves both of Jon's workflows. It leapfrogs brownfield.ai (which is just user-input site-vet scoring) by owning discovery and vet, and it pushes into the enterprise tools' territory — captive-power, MW-gated regulatory support, speed-to-power — but free, public, and indexable. And it's being built with the incoming head of DC development at a public company as your design partner, whose engineering team offered to pressure-test the data. That's a moat the gated incumbents can't buy.
